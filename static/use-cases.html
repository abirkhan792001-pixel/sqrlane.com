Use cases

Six jobs a day. Four disruptions. One desk.

Most of a forwarder’s day is the same six jobs, arriving by mail and ending on a booking. Now and then the world moves a lane and the whole board has to be re-planned. Both run through the same agents, on the same records, and every one of them stops at your approval.

This page what the desk does, one job at a time — and when a lane moves

Part one The everyday desk · six jobs

Every mail, worked on the record.

A mail arrives. The agent that owns it asks the others what it needs, the customer’s playbook is checked, and the work lands on the booking — drafted, queued, and waiting for you.

    The desk working
    Nothing is sent and nothing is written until you approve it.

    Synthetic mail from the demo inbox. What the agents do with it is computed on every run — open Workflow in the demo to see all thirteen mails, the full conversation behind each, and the corrections that teach the desk.

    Part two When a lane moves · four disruptions

    One board. Four different answers.

    The seven bookings never change — only what the world does to them. A system that gave the same answer to a strike, a closed corridor, a dry river and a fire would have learned one trick. Each decision is then handed to the desk above to carry out.
